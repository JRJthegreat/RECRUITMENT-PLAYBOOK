Saturday sends reply at 0.88% against 0.32% for every other day — a 2.7× lift. Sharper still: all three genuinely interested replies arrived Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Nothing sent on a Wednesday has produced a single reply across 620 emails.

Hold this loosely — it rests on 14 replies. A one-sided Fisher exact test on the Saturday lift gives p = 0.072, short of significance. Saturday is also confounded with sequence step: much of the Saturday volume is the first follow-up, which is the strongest step regardless of day. The honest read is a promising signal worth a deliberate test, not a settled fact.

Owners reply 4× more often than every other title combined — 3.03% against 0.76%, Fisher p = 0.013. That one clears significance, unlike the weekend finding. And the quality gap is starker than the volume gap: all three interested replies came from an owner, while 303 clinical leaders produced zero. One of them wrote back "I have no say in hiring, and I don't know who would be able to help you."

COO is not the safe second choice — it is currently the weakest bet on the board. 62 COO, Ops and Administrator contacts produced nothing at all. That is too few to call it disproven, but there is no evidence for pairing CEO with COO. Against clinical leaders alone the owner lift is 2.3× at p = 0.116, so "owner beats clinical" is the direction of travel rather than a settled result; "owner beats everything else" is the finding that holds.

Everything inside 30 days replies at 2.35%. Everything from 31 to 60 days replies at 0.88% — a 2.7× drop (p = 0.094). Past 60 days, nothing at all from 46 sends. Your 60-day window is not wrong, but its back half is close to dead weight: the 46–60 day slice is 148 leads that returned a single reply.

Stack the two rules and you get the segment worth paying for. An owner or CEO on a posting under 30 days old replies at 4.10% against 0.91% for everything else — a 4.5× lift at p = 0.005. That segment was only 23% of the list and produced 57% of every reply you got. Two of the three interested replies sit inside it; the third, Jeff at Brite Health Care, was an owner on a 36-day-old post.

What the replies expose about the lists

Every item below came out of a reply, so each one is a confirmed defect rather than a suspicion.

  • geography Three of the five Indiana repliers are Illinois companies — Northbrook, Oakbrook Terrace and Chicago. Chicago-metro spill is expected from a north-west Indiana city grid, but Northbrook and Chicago proper are not Brent's patch, and one of them replied "Stop."
  • role match HEAL360 was pitched an OBGYN nurse practitioner and answered "We are not in OB GYN Clinic." The job title came off the posting, so either the posting was mis-parsed or it was stale.
  • cadence "Please don't keep emailing us back to back." Same lead. Worth checking the gap between step 1 and step 2 on the Texas sequence — the reply came the day after the opener.
  • DM targeting Two of thirteen replies were the wrong person entirely — a Director of Rehabilitation with no hiring say, and a mailbox belonging to someone who had left. That is the practice-manager failure mode the playbook already bans, showing up one level higher.
  • enrichment TheraPeds is filed as a Houston, TX "school district" but replies from therapedsofcolorado.com and its site is therapedspartners.com. Three fields disagree on one row.
  • classification Five of thirteen repliers sit in the generic "healthcare employers" bucket, the fallback value. Employer type is driving copy, so a fallback that common is diluting the personalization on roughly a third of sends.
  • deliverability 17 bounces across the two campaigns (6 Indiana, 11 Texas) against 853 leads — about 2%. Acceptable, but Texas is running at double Indiana's rate.

How these numbers were produced

Pulled from the Instantly v2 API on 31 Jul 2026: campaign analytics, all email_type=received messages, and the full lead records for both campaigns, joined on the lead address recorded against each reply. Autoresponders were separated from human replies with subject-level and body-level rules kept deliberately apart, because "Thanks for reaching out" is an autoresponder in a subject line and a real human opener in a body — Brittany DuCharme's positive reply starts that way. The split was then reconciled against Instantly's own reply_count_automatic: 16 automatic and 14 human messages, matching the platform exactly on both campaigns. Two further messages in the received feed are Brent's own replies on threads he was CC'd into and are excluded. Reply timestamps are converted to each campaign's local timezone before any day-of-week or hour is assigned.
